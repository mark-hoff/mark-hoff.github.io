Bio

I am Assistant Professor of Spanish in the Department of Hispanic Languages & Literatures at Queens College CUNY, where I teach graduate and undergraduate courses in Spanish linguistics.

I am also Director of Language Instruction for the basic language program in Spanish and Portuguese.

I specialize in morphosyntactic and pragmatic variation, primarily in Spanish, Portuguese, and Italian. My research adopts a usage-based approach and employs experimental and corpus methods to uncover the contextual conditioning of linguistic variation. Some of my most recent work focuses on tense/mood variation, clitic expression and object marking, and pragmatic features of Argentine youth language.

Originally from northern Indiana, I attended Indiana University where I first received a dual BA in Spanish and Portuguese, with a minor in Linguistics (2011), followed by an MA in Hispanic Linguistics (2014). I then moved to Columbus, Ohio and earned a PhD in Hispanic Linguistics at The Ohio State University (2019). My dissertation was entitled Settledness and mood variation: A semantic-pragmatic analysis of Spanish future-framed adverbials.

Throughout my academic career, I have spent extensive time studying and conducting research in Argentina, and I return as often as possible.
